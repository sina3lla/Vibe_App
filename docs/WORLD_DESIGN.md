# World Design — working grammar, not a blueprint

Status: proposed design tools and examples. Agents may improve them. The confirmed product direction lives in PRODUCT_VISION.md.

## Smallest meaningful unit
An encounter connects:
1. A recognizable situation or question.
2. Something the person can change, compare, approach, or attend to.
3. An understandable response from the model.
4. A short explanation of a useful distinction, offered in context.
5. Freedom to reinterpret, try another possibility, or leave.

An encounter needs a learning purpose beyond producing a soothing effect. It must also be usable without reading a long explanation. Ambient beauty supports the interaction; it does not substitute for it.

## World structure
Let people recognize where they are, discover neighboring ideas, and return to a place. Offer an obvious first affordance without demanding that they identify their emotional state. Provide an optional plain-language index or equivalent accessible route. Movement through a world must not become a motor-skill or spatial-memory test.

Progress can mean discovering a relationship, remembering a place, or saving something useful. Do not measure healing or reward symptom disclosure. Unlocking an explanation can invite discovery, but essential help and exit controls must not be gated by achievements.

## Proposed first encounter: competing signals
Question: What feels urgent, and what do I choose to attend to?

A small field contains several legible signals. The visitor can bring one forward, create distance, or keep it present without selecting it. A second interaction explores urgency versus importance. A short explanation appears beside the chosen signal rather than taking over the screen.

The response is explicitly a demonstration of an idea. Moving a signal changes the model, not a measured bodily state. Copy can invite comparison with the person's experience without telling them what they must be feeling.

Keep the starting composition restrained. Do not simulate a panic attack through flashing, alarms, accelerating motion, or escalating visual noise. A persistent quieter presentation and simple exit remain available. Do not require breath control or inward attention.

This concept addresses one part of mental crowding. It is not yet a validated panic-support tool and should not be marketed as one.

## Other territories to investigate
- Wants and needs: explore the difference between desire, obligation, hesitation, and expectation. Sensation offers information alongside context.
- Boundaries: experiment with distance, permission, and preference without defining one universal correct boundary.
- Social interpretation: compare what was observed with possible explanations and available responses.
- Rest and ambition: explore wanting something while also acknowledging limits, doubt, or a need to pause.

These are a creative backlog, not a minimum launch map. Do not implement them all in the first cycle.

## Content record for each encounter
Record the intended distinction, interactions, exact explanatory copy, source/status of factual claims, limits of the metaphor, accessibility alternatives, and exit behavior. Label unverified content as draft in review records; do not present it as established fact to users.

## Questions for evaluating a prototype
Can someone discover an action without a tutorial? Can they explain what that action illustrates? Can they reject the interpretation? Does an explanation arrive when useful? Can they stay with their real experience instead of managing a complicated game? What would make them return out of curiosity?

## Two color schemes
Design bright and dark palettes together using shared semantic color roles. Preserve the world's identity, hierarchy, and readability in both, including interactive states, contextual explanations, settings, and system bars. Do not implement the bright scheme as a simple color inversion. The precise palettes remain a creative choice.

Default to silent system adaptation. Expose only the requested Settings control: Design → System, Bright, Dark; no explanatory box is required. A selected override takes precedence over subsequent system-theme changes.
